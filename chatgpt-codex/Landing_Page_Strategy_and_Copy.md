# Chris Demetriou — Landing Page Strategy & Copy

**Status:** Approval required before implementation. This document fulfils the creative brief; no site code has been changed.

## 1. Critique of the current landing page

The existing page is a confident, highly produced **audio-production portfolio**, not an enterprise-technology landing page. Its title, schema, hero, showreel-first navigation, SoundCloud player, creative credits, and contact framing all position Chris for broadcast and branded-audio work. That is incompatible with the proposed primary audience of enterprise recruiters, hiring managers, CIOs, and CTOs.

What works:

- The dark, premium visual treatment and a clear personal point of view feel considered.
- The work is tangible: the showreel, credits and case studies make the creative career credible.
- The site already has responsive layouts, labelled controls, and strong visual craft to reuse selectively.

What needs to change:

- **Positioning:** technology leadership needs to be the default story, with creative work as reinforcing proof of judgement, not the front door.
- **Information hierarchy:** a long audio player and many creative categories delay the answer to the recruiter’s first question: “What outcomes can this person create here?”
- **Evidence:** replace tool/genre density with clear operating strengths, representative transformation contexts, and a concise career narrative. Do not invent metrics, employers, or credentials.
- **Visual language:** the current studio-console / retro-TV motifs are memorable but too specialised for the target role. Move to a restrained enterprise-system aesthetic.
- **Technical foundation:** the single large stylesheet has many overlapping responsive and animation rules. The future build should use a small component system, semantic landmarks, visible keyboard focus, and a reduced-motion mode.

## 2. Strategy

### Positioning statement

Chris Demetriou is an enterprise technology operations leader who makes complex change safer by exposing dependencies, challenging assumptions, connecting the right people, and ensuring operational readiness before delivery becomes disruption.

### Audience and message

| Audience | Their question | Landing-page answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CIO / CTO | “Will this person see the system-level risk?” | He works across engineering, infrastructure, networks, applications, vendors and operations. |
| Hiring manager | “Can they move delivery forward without creating noise?” | He creates clarity, ownership and readiness at the seams between teams. |
| Enterprise recruiter | “What is the role category?” | Enterprise Technology Operations Manager; a cross-functional operator, not support or a generic PM. |

### Narrative

1. Establish the cost of missed dependencies.
2. Name Chris’s specific role in preventing that failure mode.
3. Make the operating model concrete.
4. Show that the creative career is additional evidence of careful listening, synthesis and delivery—not a competing proposition.
5. Invite a relevant conversation.

### Success criteria

- A visitor understands the technology proposition within one screen.
- The next action is a conversation or LinkedIn profile visit, not a media-player interaction.
- Creative Media remains discoverable and distinctive without displacing the technology story.

## 3. Information architecture

1. Header — wordmark, Technology / Creative Media tabs, `Start a conversation` CTA.
2. Hero — central proposition and two low-friction CTAs.
3. Operating principle — why enterprise change fails; why Chris is valuable.
4. How Chris operates — four-system model.
5. Where he creates leverage — cross-functional dependency map.
6. What good looks like — outcome-led readiness principles, deliberately metric-free until verified evidence is supplied.
7. Creative Media — secondary proof, tab-specific and compact.
8. About / working style — concise personal context.
9. Contact — clear, professional conversion point.

## 6. UX rationale

- The default tab is Enterprise Technology because it serves the primary hiring audience immediately.
- The tab is a content lens, not a hidden site: direct URLs, page titles and accessible state must be supported for both views.
- The hero uses one clear primary action and one credibility action. Avoid a downloadable CV until the user supplies an approved document and distribution preference.
- The dependency map gives the positioning a visual explanation without claiming unverified project results.
- The creative tab earns curiosity and adds dimensionality after the core case is made.

## 7. Visual direction — Microsoft-calibre enterprise product

The experience should feel at home beside a premium Microsoft technology product: confident rather than flashy; clear rather than minimal for its own sake; warm and human without becoming informal. It must not copy Microsoft marks, typography, imagery, components or product UI.

### Design principles

- **Clarity earns trust.** Use a bright, cloud-white canvas, deep ink text and generous whitespace. The reader should immediately know what Chris does, where to look next, and why it matters.
- **Precision signals capability.** Build on a quiet spacing grid, crisp rules, highly legible type, aligned cards and intentional responsive behaviour. Nothing should feel decorative or improvised.
- **Humanity keeps it credible.** Use a restrained portrait or an abstract “people and systems” visual—not generic boardroom stock imagery. Write in direct, plain language.
- **Depth is functional.** Use very soft shadows, layered pale-blue surfaces and a single Microsoft-adjacent azure signal colour only to indicate interaction, key terms and system paths.
- **Technology is understandable.** Translate enterprise complexity into an elegant dependency graph, readable labels and short explanations—not a sci-fi network illustration.

### Palette and typography

- Foundation: cloud white (`#F7F8FA`) with white surfaces; deep charcoal (`#1B1B1B`) for text.
- Signal: an azure-blue in the `#0078D4` family, used sparingly; a pale-blue tint for information surfaces; one subtle teal or violet accent only if hierarchy needs it.
- Type: a contemporary humanist sans-serif with strong numerals and excellent screen rendering. Use a single family with a restrained display scale; do not use condensed, techno or retro type.
- Shapes: modest 8–12 px radii, fine neutral borders, no glassmorphism, no heavy gradients, no neon, no grain, and no visual effects that obscure content.

### Composition

The hero should pair the proposition with a calm dependency field: labelled nodes for Engineering, Network, Applications, Vendor and Operations, connected by precise thin paths. One path can become blue on hover or keyboard focus to demonstrate a dependency. The page should move from broad strategic statement to clear operating model to contact, with broad horizontal bands and editorial breathing room.

Creative Media uses the same system—white space, ink, azure and clean cards—with a small waveform rhythm as a secondary motif. It should feel like another capability area of the same professional, not an entirely separate brand.

## 8. CTA strategy

The primary CTA throughout is `Start a conversation`, opening the existing contact flow or a concise mailto/contact form. The secondary CTA is `Connect on LinkedIn`. These are appropriate for a hiring audience and do not over-promise a vacancy-specific action.

Do not use “Hire me”, “Book a call”, or media-first CTAs in the technology path. Repeat the primary CTA after the operating model and in the closing section only.

## 9. Animation recommendations

- A 600–900 ms, low-contrast reveal of the dependency graph on first view.
- On hover/focus, reveal one dependency route and a short explanatory label.
- Tab changes cross-fade and move content 8–12 px; retain keyboard focus and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Use no autoplay video, looping marquee, scroll-jacking, or decorative motion that competes with reading.
