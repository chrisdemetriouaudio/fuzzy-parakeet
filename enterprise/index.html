Enterprise Technology Delivery

The conduit between delivery and engineering

Put an engineer and whoever owns the date in the same room and both will leave confident they agreed. They usually haven’t. One was describing a system; the other heard a date.

I work in that gap — asking the technical questions the delivery side can’t, then translating the answers into dependency, risk and date.

So nobody finds out at the cutover window that “fine” meant “untested”.

How I work

Principles, not process

  • Systems Thinking

    Every platform is a set of relationships. The failure is rarely inside a component — it’s in the space between two of them.

  • Engineering Collaboration

    Specialists don’t need managing. They need the right people in the room and the right question on the table.

  • Technical Readiness

    Ready means demonstrated: a tested VPN, a verified firewall rule, an integration that has actually exchanged data.

  • Operational Readiness

    A platform that works isn’t finished. Monitoring, handover and acceptance decide whether it keeps working.

  • Production Change

    Cutovers reward preparation and punish optimism. Most of the work happens in the weeks before the window.

  • Technical Communication

    Translating between engineering detail and delivery consequence, without diluting either one.

  • Risk Reduction

    The cheapest defect is the one found in a workshop, not at 2am on a bridge call.
