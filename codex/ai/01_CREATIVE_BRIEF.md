Creative Brief

Purpose

This document defines the creative direction for ChrisDemetriou.com Version 2.

It should be read after 00_PROJECT_VISION.md.

This document explains how the vision should be expressed through UX, copywriting, interaction design and visual storytelling.

It is the authoritative reference for every design decision.

⸻

Project Objective

Design a premium digital experience that positions Chris Demetriou as an exceptional Enterprise Technology Operations Manager.

The objective is not to showcase experience.

The objective is to demonstrate how Chris thinks.

Every interaction should reinforce his ability to navigate enterprise complexity, identify dependencies, reduce risk and improve technology delivery.

⸻

Primary Audience

The homepage is designed primarily for:

* Enterprise Technology Recruiters
* Hiring Managers
* Heads of Technology
* Enterprise Technology Directors
* Enterprise Applications Leaders
* Technology Operations Leaders
* CIOs
* CTOs

Secondary audiences include:

* Enterprise Architects
* Engineering Managers
* Technical Colleagues
* Creative industry contacts

⸻

What Visitors Should Think

Within the first 30 seconds:

“This person understands enterprise technology.”

Within two minutes:

“He understands problems we deal with every day.”

Before leaving:

“I’d like to meet him.”

⸻

The Hero Philosophy

Never begin with:

* a biography
* a job title
* a headshot
* a list of skills

Begin with insight.

The homepage opens with:

Enterprise technology rarely fails because of the technology.

It fails when dependencies are missed, assumptions go unchallenged and the right conversations happen too late.

That’s where I come in.

Only after the visitor understands the problem should Chris gradually be introduced.

His portrait should appear naturally as the user scrolls.

The story comes before the person.

⸻

Homepage Narrative

The landing page should tell a story.

Chapter 1

Recognise the problem.

⸻

Chapter 2

Explain why enterprise technology is complex.

⸻

Chapter 3

Demonstrate how Chris thinks.

⸻

Chapter 4

Show how Chris helps organisations succeed.

⸻

Chapter 5

Support the message with experience.

⸻

Chapter 6

Invite conversation.

⸻

Positioning

The website should consistently reinforce:

Enterprise Technology Operations Manager

This positioning should appear naturally throughout the copy.

Avoid repeatedly listing job titles.

Demonstrate the role through examples.

⸻

Differentiate Without Attacking

The website should never criticise Project Managers.

Instead communicate:

Project Managers help deliver projects.

Chris helps ensure enterprise technology is ready to be delivered.

These roles strengthen one another.

⸻

Information Flow

One of the signature interactions on the website.

The visitor should see information flowing between enterprise disciplines.

Technical information arrives.

Chris analyses it.

Dependencies are identified.

Information leaves in forms appropriate for different audiences.

Examples:

Technical input:

* VLAN Required
* CAB Approval
* API Dependency
* Rollback Plan
* Identity Mapping

Business output:

* Network change required before deployment.
* Governance approval needed.
* Integration dependency identified.
* Recovery strategy agreed.
* User access impact confirmed.

The technology does not change.

Understanding does.

⸻

Interactive Enterprise Diagrams

Use diagrams instead of paragraphs wherever possible.

Examples include:

* dependency maps
* information flow
* release readiness
* change lifecycle
* operational readiness
* enterprise ecosystem

Animations should educate.

Never decorate.

⸻

Technology and Creative Media

The homepage contains two premium tabs.

* Enterprise Technology
* Creative Media

Enterprise Technology is selected by default.

Technology receives approximately 70% emphasis.

Creative Media receives approximately 30%.

Creative Media demonstrates another dimension of Chris’s professional life.

It should reinforce creativity, communication and craftsmanship without diluting the technology positioning.

⸻

Visual Language

The website should feel like a premium SaaS product.

Primary inspiration:

* Apple
* Linear
* Vercel
* GitHub
* Raycast
* Notion

Characteristics:

* elegant
* minimal
* editorial
* technically credible
* calm
* spacious

Avoid:

* stock portfolio layouts
* corporate templates
* excessive gradients
* visual clutter
* unnecessary decoration

⸻

Theme

Dark Mode is the primary experience.

The visual identity should be built around a carefully designed dark theme.

Light Mode should also be available.

Requirements:

* theme toggle
* remember preference
* smooth transition
* both themes intentionally designed

Dark Mode should feel closer to professional creative software than a traditional dark website.

⸻

Cognitive Clarity

The website must reduce cognitive load.

Design for scanning.

Not reading.

Use:

* large headings
* generous spacing
* cards
* diagrams
* visual hierarchy
* progressive disclosure
* concise paragraphs

Every section should communicate one clear idea.

⸻

Reading Experience

Provide premium reading controls.

Include:

Focus Mode

Reduces visual noise.

Minimises distractions.

Optimises long-form reading.

⸻

Read Aloud

Allow visitors to listen to page content.

Provide:

* play
* pause
* resume
* stop

Highlight the text being spoken where practical.

⸻

Reading Preferences

Allow users to customise:

* text size
* line spacing
* paragraph spacing
* reduced motion
* reading width

Remember preferences between visits.

⸻

Accessibility

Accessibility should be integrated into the design.

Requirements include:

* WCAG 2.2 AA
* semantic HTML
* keyboard navigation
* visible focus states
* reduced motion support
* colour contrast compliance

Accessibility should improve the experience for everyone.

⸻

Photography

Photography should feel authentic.

Avoid:

* stock imagery
* folded arms
* staged office scenes
* exaggerated corporate smiles

Preferred style:

* editorial
* documentary
* premium
* authentic

Photography placeholders should be included during implementation.

⸻

Motion Design

Motion exists to improve understanding.

Examples:

* portrait reveal
* information flow
* dependency activation
* section transitions
* subtle hover interactions

Avoid animation that exists only for decoration.

⸻

Privacy

Include:

* Cookie consent
* Privacy Policy
* Cookie Policy
* Accessibility Statement

Cookie consent should be:

* elegant
* minimal
* GDPR conscious
* consistent with the visual language

⸻

SEO, AI & ATS

The website should communicate clearly to:

* people
* search engines
* AI assistants
* applicant tracking systems

Optimise using:

* semantic HTML
* structured headings
* descriptive metadata
* JSON-LD
* Open Graph
* accessible markup

Never write for algorithms at the expense of humans.

⸻

Copywriting Principles

Write like someone who has spent years inside enterprise technology.

Avoid:

* buzzwords
* clichés
* generic competency statements
* inflated claims

Instead:

* explain
* demonstrate
* teach
* simplify

Confidence comes from insight.

Not self-promotion.

⸻

Show, Don’t Tell

Replace paragraphs with:

* diagrams
* timelines
* relationship maps
* enterprise workflows
* interactive explanations

The website should communicate visually wherever possible.

⸻

Success Criteria

The creative direction succeeds when:

* recruiters understand Chris’s positioning within seconds
* hiring managers recognise enterprise systems thinking
* CTOs see someone who understands organisational complexity
* Creative Media enhances the overall brand
* visitors remember the experience
* every interaction supports the central narrative

Nothing should exist simply because it looks impressive.

Everything should exist because it strengthens the story.
