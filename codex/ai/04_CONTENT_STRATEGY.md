Content Strategy

Purpose

This document defines the content strategy for ChrisDemetriou.com Version 2.

It explains what should be communicated, in what order, and why.

Every page should educate before it persuades.

The goal is not to impress visitors.

The goal is to help them understand Chris’s value.

⸻

Content Philosophy

Most personal websites answer:

“What have I done?”

This website answers:

“How do I think?”

Every page should reinforce that distinction.

⸻

Primary Story

The website should communicate one central idea:

Chris helps enterprise organisations navigate technology complexity by identifying dependencies, challenging assumptions and ensuring the right technical conversations happen before implementation.

Everything else supports that message.

⸻

Homepage Journey

1. The Insight

The opening should immediately challenge conventional thinking.

The visitor should recognise a truth about enterprise technology before learning anything about Chris.

⸻

2. The Problem

Explain why enterprise technology becomes difficult.

Focus on:

* complexity
* dependencies
* communication
* operational readiness
* change

Not technology itself.

⸻

3. The Solution

Introduce Chris.

Demonstrate how he approaches enterprise technology differently.

Avoid generic competency statements.

⸻

4. Show How Chris Thinks

This is the most important section.

Use:

* information flow
* dependency diagrams
* enterprise ecosystems
* decision flows
* operational readiness examples

Show rather than tell.

⸻

5. Enterprise Experience

Support the narrative with experience.

Do not list responsibilities.

Explain:

Situation

Challenge

Approach

Outcome

Learning

Every example should demonstrate thinking.

⸻

6. Creative Media

Present Creative Media as another professional discipline.

Not a hobby.

Not an unrelated passion.

Explain how it demonstrates:

* communication
* creativity
* craftsmanship
* attention to detail
* technical excellence

Technology remains the primary story.

⸻

7. Call to Action

Invite conversation.

Avoid aggressive sales language.

Suggested tone:

“If you’re building complex enterprise technology and value someone who naturally thinks about dependencies, operational readiness and cross-functional technology alignment, I’d love to talk.”

⸻

Tone of Voice

Write like an experienced enterprise professional.

The tone should be:

* calm
* thoughtful
* intelligent
* approachable
* confident
* humble

Avoid:

* hype
* exaggeration
* buzzwords
* consultancy clichés

⸻

Copy Principles

Every paragraph should answer:

Why should the reader care?

Every sentence should earn its place.

If a paragraph can become a diagram, make it a diagram.

If a list can become an animation, consider an animation.

⸻

Headlines

Headlines should communicate insight.

Not categories.

Instead of:

“My Experience”

Use:

“The biggest technology risks usually aren’t technical.”

Instead of:

“Stakeholder Management”

Use:

“Different teams. One shared understanding.”

Every heading should make the visitor curious.

⸻

Enterprise Case Studies

Every case study should follow a consistent structure.

Situation

What was changing?

⸻

Challenge

Why was it difficult?

⸻

Dependencies

What made the change complex?

⸻

My Thinking

What questions did I ask?

What assumptions did I challenge?

What conversations needed to happen?

⸻

Outcome

What improved?

⸻

Reflection

What did this reinforce about enterprise technology?

⸻

Enterprise Topics

Future articles should focus on enterprise thinking rather than technology products.

Examples:

* Why enterprise technology rarely fails because of the technology.
* Hidden dependencies in enterprise change.
* Operational readiness is everyone’s responsibility.
* The questions every technology team should ask before implementation.
* Why communication is an architectural concern.
* Lessons from large-scale enterprise delivery.

⸻

Testimonials

Testimonials should reinforce:

* judgement
* calmness
* technical understanding
* collaboration
* enterprise thinking
* professionalism

Avoid generic praise.

⸻

Calls to Action

CTAs should feel conversational.

Avoid:

“Hire Me”

Instead use:

“Let’s talk.”

“Discuss your next technology challenge.”

“Connect.”

The website should invite discussion rather than promote itself.

⸻

Recruiter Journey

A recruiter should understand within one minute:

* target role
* level of experience
* differentiator
* next action

Everything should reduce ambiguity.

⸻

Hiring Manager Journey

A hiring manager should recognise:

* enterprise systems thinking
* operational maturity
* technical credibility
* calm decision-making
* organisational awareness

The website should make them want to continue the conversation.

⸻

CTO Journey

A CTO should recognise:

* someone who understands enterprise complexity
* someone who reduces organisational risk
* someone who asks the right questions
* someone who strengthens technical teams

The emphasis should be on judgement rather than technical jargon.

⸻

Creative Media Journey

Creative Media should communicate:

* technical excellence
* creativity
* attention to detail
* communication
* professionalism

It should broaden Chris’s profile without distracting from Enterprise Technology.

⸻

Content Checklist

Every page should answer:

Why does this page exist?

Who is it for?

What should the visitor think afterwards?

What action should they take?

If the answers are unclear, rewrite the page.

⸻

Final Principle

The website should never feel like someone describing their career.

It should feel like someone sharing the lessons they have learned from sixteen years of helping enterprise technology succeed.
